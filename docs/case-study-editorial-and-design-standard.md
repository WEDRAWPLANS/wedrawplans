# WEDRAWPLANS — Case-Study Editorial, Design and Lead Standard
Updated: 1 November 2026
Scope: 24 approved-project case-study pages, approvals hub, relevant borough/town/service integrations. Preview branch only until client approval.

## Business objective
Make WEDRAWPLANS a trusted, easy-to-contact architectural drawing and planning consultancy for London and the surrounding M25 areas. Target at least 10 qualified enquiries per week; measure enquiry source, conversion and follow-up rather than promise ranking outcomes.

## Permanent presentation direction
- Professional business consultancy tone: clear, assured, precise, restrained.
- Never use the adjective "real" to market projects or approvals; avoid empty phrases such as "flagship", "proof engine", "leading", "unbeatable" and exaggerated claims.
- Preferred wording: "Approved extension", "Planning permission granted", "Selected planning projects", "Project overview", "Scope of work", "Planning outcome", "Architectural drawings", "Council application reference".
- Do not label an approved proposal as built, constructed or completed unless the project records specifically confirm that status.
- Never publish fabricated reviews, approval percentages, project photographs or council endorsements.
- Use WEDRAWPLANS/Barnet design language, but refined: white and warm off-white surfaces, muted teal/turquoise accents, modest borders, readable type, ample white space. Avoid large black/dark background blocks, cluttered panels and excessive uppercase promotional copy.
- Maintain clean hierarchy on desktop and mobile. Do not repeat the same descriptions within a page.

## Standard case-study information architecture
1. Professional navigation and breadcrumb.
2. Clear, descriptive planning/project headline and locality.
3. Planning outcome, council, application number, year and project type.
4. One concise summary followed by specific, non-duplicative scope details.
5. Project-specific drawing, plan, render or photograph where available and permitted to publish. No generic photo represented as that project.
6. Planning decision explanation, WEDRAWPLANS role, drawing identifiers and material conditions when documented.
7. Relevant householder service and local borough/town links.
8. A tasteful "Get your fixed-fee quote" CTA, clearly visible near the top and at the end.
9. 2–3 genuinely related project links, selected by work type and geography, not arbitrary rotation.
10. Unique page metadata and matching Article/Breadcrumb JSON-LD. Do not use markup that asserts unsupported facts.

## Approvals-hub architecture
- Central /case-studies page searchable by town, council and project type.
- Show 24 selected approved projects with descriptive titles and succinct scope information.
- Use user-friendly filters and light project cards.
- Prioritise extensions without obscuring loft conversions, Lawful Development Certificates, flat conversions, mixed use, heritage, new residential schemes, solar/PV-related design evidence or dropped-kerb work.
- Acknowledge that an approval does not guarantee approval of a similar future proposal.

## Enquiry & measurement requirements (next stage)
- Clear fixed-fee quote path on every case study and relevant borough/service page.
- Enquiry service choices should cover house extensions, loft conversions, planning drawings, Building Regulations, LDCs, outbuildings, solar panels/PV, dropped kerb, flat conversions, new build, commercial, heritage and retrospective planning.
- Record source page, project/service and borough/town on submissions, then reconcile leads with WEDRAWPLANS Job Book and ClickUp.
- Track meaningful conversion measures: qualified enquiry rate, quote requests, instructed projects and lead/source performance, not only page views.
- Keep form simple on mobile and ask only for information needed to respond.

## Delivery controls

- Preview deployments should not be indexed.
- Never use ChatGPT Work or additional paid credits without explicit approval.
- Before proposing launch: check wording, planning evidence, project-specific imagery, responsive design, accessibility, navigation, forms, schema, indexing settings and build results.
